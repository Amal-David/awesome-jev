# Help the right builders discover awesome-jev

The objective is useful repeat visits and real contributions; stars are a secondary signal. These are proposed experiments, not promises of reach or star counts.

## Lead with a problem the directory solves

Positioning: **“Find the source code behind the Jev demo you just watched.”** A giant unfiltered link count is less useful than a short route from a demo to a concrete implementation, its reusable pattern, and its limitations.

Keep the five-demo homepage focused. Make one entry genuinely better each time: a primary code link, an explanation of what Jev decides, an identified mock mode, or a corrected license. Submitters should not need to understand the generator to suggest a resource, so use the resource issue form.

## First distribution experiment: a demonstration-led X post

Post from the maintainer's own account after reviewing the wording. Link original creators' posts instead of downloading and re-uploading their clips without permission. Do not imply affiliation or endorsement.

Draft:

> The Jev demo is cool. Finding the actual code shouldn't take another 20 minutes.
>
> I’m collecting the demos **with their repos, reusable patterns, skills, and caveats**:
> voice-controlled browsing, DOM agents, desktop control, and coding-agent supervision.
>
> Watch → inspect the code → steal the pattern, not the benchmark claim.
>
> https://github.com/Amal-David/awesome-jev
>
> Built something with Jev? Send the original demo and source link.

Follow with a small number of substantive examples, each crediting its creator and explaining a different pattern. Avoid mass tagging, automated replies, or repeated link drops. Invite corrections rather than asking strangers for stars.

## Second experiment: one useful technical article

The accompanying `DEVTO_DRAFT.md` is an AI-assisted educational draft, not a published post. Add the maintainer's own observations or reproduced experiment before publishing. Teach candidate generation, stale-state checks, and explicit execution boundaries. Link primary implementations because they support the explanation, not as a backlink scheme.

DEV explicitly requires AI disclosure, factual checking, and authors who understand their topic; it disallows AI-assisted promotion/clout-seeking, backlink campaigns, and AI-generated comments. A post whose main goal is repository stars is not appropriate there. Read the [current policy](https://dev.to/guidelines-for-ai-assisted-articles-on-dev) before publishing. Do not automate comment outreach.

A maintainer-owned blog or repository article can carry a direct project announcement, with honest authorship and source attribution. Cross-post only where the community's rules allow it.

## Third experiment: collaborate with people already building

Ask a few featured creators for a correction to their entry when there is a genuine reason to contact them. Prefer a normal conversation in an existing thread over a promotional issue on their repository. Offer a useful patch, missing documentation, or a reproducible test instead of a reciprocal-star arrangement.

Apply to a relevant community directory only after reading its contribution requirements. Some Awesome collections require a maturity period or a particular license/format. Do not mass-open near-identical promotional PRs.

## Fourth experiment: make returning worthwhile

Publish a small “new demos and patterns” digest only when there are meaningful additions. Distinguish new discoveries from newly verified source code; do not turn every four-hour metadata refresh into an announcement. A changelog with five useful additions is preferable to dozens of timestamp-only posts.

## Measure without inventing conversions

After each distribution experiment, compare GitHub's available unique visitor/referrer and clone data, star growth, and resource submissions over a consistent window. Where exact referral attribution is unavailable, report an association rather than claiming causation. Keep dates and denominators; avoid claiming a conversion rate from incompatible visitor and star counts.

Never buy stars, use bots or sockpuppets, offer star-for-star rewards, or spam communities. None of this work posts to X, creates outreach messages, or enrolls the repository in a growth service automatically.
